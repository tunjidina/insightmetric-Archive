# Brand

Name, tagline, vision and philosophy. This page covers how InsightMetric is described; it
does not cover how its assets are produced.

---

## Name

**InsightMetric.** One word, two capitals, singular.

| | |
|---|---|
| Correct | InsightMetric |
| Wrong | InsightMetrics, Insight Metric, Insightmetric, INSIGHTMETRIC |

Lowercase `insightmetric` is used only where casing is not a choice — a repository name, a
package, a URL.

It is a project, not a company. Avoid possessives that imply otherwise.

## Tagline

> **Tech Trends, Minus the Noise.**

Title case, always with the closing full stop. Used once per surface, alongside the name —
a header, a page footer, the first line of a post. Not a section heading, and not repeated
for emphasis.

## How to describe it

Use this wording. Please do not paraphrase it into a fourth version.

**Short:**

> InsightMetric is a lightweight daily trend-intelligence dashboard that tracks emerging
> signals across AI, cybersecurity, data science and technology, and shows which ones look
> likely to still be around tomorrow.

**One line:**

> Daily trend intelligence — clearer signals, less noise.

## Logo

The mark is called **Signal Map**: a minimal world map carrying five points of light for the
domains the product watches — AI, cloud, cybersecurity, developer tools and open source.
Clean lines, a quiet monochrome palette, one accent colour.

It comes in a few forms — the full lockup with the tagline, a compact version for small
spaces, and a single point of light for avatars and browser tabs, since a world map is
illegible at that size. Light and dark versions exist; only the type colour differs between
them.

**When using it:**

- Leave clear space around it. Nothing crowds the mark.
- Do not recolour, rotate, stretch, outline, or add effects.
- Do not stack it vertically or rebuild it in another arrangement.
- The map is decorative. It is not a map of anything, and the five points are placed for
  balance — they do not mark locations.

## Vision

A daily reading of the technology landscape that is short enough to actually read, honest
about what it does not know, and gets better the longer it runs.

## Philosophy

> **Simple tools → powerful insights.**

The product grows over time and improves as its history grows. Small, understandable
components, arranged so each can be improved without rebuilding the rest.

**Start with the simplest thing that works.** Something you can explain in a sentence beats
something elaborate you cannot. Complexity earns its place by making the product measurably
better.

**Say what you do not know.** Estimates come with a sense of confidence, and the
documentation states where the ground is thin. That is a feature of the product, not a
disclaimer bolted onto it.

**Build so it can be replaced.** Everything here is a foundation, not a ceiling.

## Voice

The writing is part of the brand.

- **Plain, specific, unhurried.** Say the thing. Give the number.
- **Name the limits.** The note about sample data exists because it would be easy and
  dishonest to leave out. Stating a limit is what makes the rest credible.
- **No hype vocabulary.** Not *revolutionary*, *powerful*, *seamless*, *cutting-edge*,
  *game-changing*, *unlock*, *leverage*. The product's argument is that something small and
  legible beats something large and opaque; the writing should sound like it believes that.
- **British spelling** — *behaviour*, *licence*, *summarise*, *modelling*.
- **No emoji in prose.** The traffic-light badges in the app are interface, not writing.
- **Never claim a capability that does not exist yet.** If it is planned, say it is planned.
